## Current owner-approved motion and visual refinements

**MANDATORY — descriptive labels, graphic consistency and complete enumerations (owner feedback, BM-006, 27 November 2026):** Place descriptive text and component labels beside, above or below artwork, in dedicated clear space. Do not overlap or superimpose descriptive text on visual objects, connectors or other artwork unless it intentionally belongs within that graphic (for example a readable UI field or a labeled diagram surface) and remains fully legible throughout the animation. Check complete glyph bounds and graphic extents, including pins, shadows, strokes, connector paths and entrance offsets; inspect actual intermediate and settled decoded frames and fix collisions at source. Reuse coherent visual assets and preserve their proportions, detail and styling across scenes when they represent the same concept; resize proportionally rather than stretching a chip or other object. Distinct shapes are acceptable when a genuinely different role or verified design warrants them. If a scene explicitly represents a numbered set of scripts, languages or other items, verify the actual members against authoritative evidence and animate/show all of them legibly when requested; never confuse scripts with languages or invent missing members. Use a layout or sequential reveal that gives each item sufficient reading time and space, without crowding or connector/text overlap. For diagram connectors, route lines outside the full animated text bounds, including descenders and stroke width; verify connector clearance during every reveal and settled state with geometric regression checks. For rotating groups of labels, retain the final group through the outgoing transition.

**MANDATORY — immediate animated opening hero (owner feedback, BM-006, 27 November 2026):** Scene 1 must establish its core subject in the opening frame, without waiting for a spoken cue. Begin the primary graphic's directional entrance at t=0 with a visible initial state, smoothly increasing opacity and settling into position over about 0.48s. Do not simply display the finished static object, delay its entrance, or leave the main visual area empty. Headlines retain their immediate typewriter animation; secondary evidence, labels and later facts retain acoustic-cue timing. Record this opening exception in the adjusted cue map and check decoded opening frames at 0, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5s.


**MANDATORY — viewer-facing text only and graphic clearance (owner feedback, 27 November 2026, BM-006):** Do not render source references, research notes, QA annotations, internal helper labels, placeholder labels, or meta text such as “Illustrative pronunciation” in story frames, captions, or final video. Keep such information exclusively in private review/QA records and source metadata; retain genuine editorial qualifiers (e.g. “reported”, “potential applications”) when needed for factual accuracy. This applies to every storyboard and animated production, not just the offending scene. Inspect all actual frame states, including partial typewriter text, and use source-level validation of visible labels. Reserve clearance below complete illustrations including pins, shadows, strokes and animated offsets; ensure model-size labels, central diagram labels and lower application labels never crowd their associated graphics. Verify settled and transitional frames before presenting a revision.


**MANDATORY — immediate headline, anticipatory visual cues and clear labels (owner instruction, 26 November 2026):** Begin the scene kicker and main topic headline animation at scene start, not at spoken phrase onset; second headline line may stagger slightly (about 0.18s). Maintain the slightly slower editorial typewriter (roughly 18–20 characters/sec). Start supporting graphics, counters, and related labels approximately 0.35 seconds before the relevant actual acoustic cue, clamped to scene start; adjust by beat as needed so the subject becomes recognizable when spoken, without premature disclosure of later facts. Captions remain exactly word-aligned. Store original and adjusted cue maps; avoid applying the lead twice. Reserve separate text and illustration areas, check full label bounds plus moving artwork extents during entrances and settled frames, and leave visible clearance rather than allowing text/art or headline/callout collisions. Prefer technically credible detailed illustrations over cartoon-like geometry. Retain musical resolution and the one-second silent final hold. This refines the prior narration-led cue rule.

**MANDATORY — narration-led visual cues and resolved ending (owner instruction 26 November 2026):** Slow editorial cursorless typewriter animation slightly: target approximately 20 characters per actual second (previous BM-005 implementation 25), with a gentle ~0.48s directional entrance; adapt label length and choreography to the spoken beat rather than clipping text or rushing it. Keep actual word-follow captions independently tied to the acoustic timing. After final narration arrives, bind each meaningful editorial reveal, counter, machinery/diagram entrance and relationship to the relevant phrase/word onset from hash-verified WhisperX alignment; do not simply scale preset scene timings to narration duration or pre-reveal later facts. Background and fixed brand chrome may establish context, and already revealed subjects may continue subtle motion. Store explicit per-scene cue maps, inspect decoded frames just before/at/after key cues and ensure labels finish legibly. End on a completed branded composition, no new spoken tagline or unrelated end card. Preserve the whole last spoken phrase, give the music a short natural resolving fade after it (~0.8s starting at final narration WAV end as a default, adapt to music), then hold the final composition for one full second after all audible audio ends. The intentional silent end hold must be included in planned frame count and timestamps; only the explicitly documented final hold may contain identical consecutive frames. No unintended freezes, abrupt audio cutoffs, early music silence or truncation. Verify audio fade, tail silence, final progress completion and decoded frame counts including hold. Apply from BM-005; no retroactive changes to published releases.

**MANDATORY — scene-level mixed themes (owner approved 26 November 2026):** Plan themes by narrative purpose, not just dominant upload color. Aim approximately 50% Warm Ivory, 30% Deep Charcoal, 20% Classic Saffron across scenes over multiple videos; these are editorial targets, not rigid quotas. Include at least one full saffron feature scene per video where visually appropriate; document a reason if omitted. Prefer saffron for major announcements, landmark figures or a strong closing reveal; ivory for facts, comparisons and instructional diagrams; charcoal for cinematic machinery, space, technology and contrast-rich environments. Technical mechanisms can use ivory or charcoal. Vary sequence with story and avoid forced predictable alternation. All scenes still need unique contextual backgrounds and meaningful motion; changing only color is insufficient. Check actual rendered contrast of all text, labels, captions and progress tracks. Saffron uses dark readable editorial ink, ivory progress fill and sufficiently dark neutral caption backing when needed for ivory/peach caption legibility. This supersedes the old 65/25/10 upload-dominant mix. Do not re-render previously approved releases solely for rotation.

**Mandatory continuous orbital motion and render completeness (25 November 2026):** Preserve spacecraft identity, scale and orientation continuously through trajectory phases; use analytic or interpolated subpixel motion with matched velocity at joins, not integer-indexed position samples or abruptly swapped sprites. A travelling path endpoint must use the same position function as the spacecraft and continue drawing if motion continues. Keep orbiting elements moving after assembly, with stable spacing and clearances. Inspect actual decoded intermediate frames and the last frame. For segmented renders, require every subprocess to exit successfully and verify each segment's decoded frame count before concatenating. Reject incomplete output even if audio duration or container duration looks correct. Verify exact planned video frame count, consecutive PTS spacing, A/V durations, full decode and repeated-frame runs across the entire output. Near-static intentional designs must be distinguished from actual frozen or missing frames.

**MANDATORY — source-grounded visual assets (owner approved 25 November 2026):** Use accurate geographic datasets and credible spacecraft geometry where relevant; distinguish generic schematic hardware and conceptual orbits from actual mission assets. For scientific sensor comparisons prefer authentic, credited observations, ideally matched area and comparable scale, with verified provenance and usage rights. Use verified current official company/institution marks when useful and readable; plain text is the fallback when assets cannot be verified or acquired. Do not invent brand marks or pass synthetic sensor imagery off as observations. Render actual storyboard frames from editable production code; no generated storyboard/contact-sheet substitute. Missing requested assets are review blockers, not permission to advance to video production. Retain existing approved layout, captions and motion rules.

**MANDATORY — Constructive Motion Design (owner approved 25 November 2026):** Animate the formation of meaningful relationships, not merely the movement of finished graphics. When appropriate for the story: (1) timeline/route/path starts at an anchor, types its label/supporting text, draws progressively with a travelling endpoint, settles at destination, then types destination information; (2) connected systems establish the connector/foundation first, then attach components sequentially in a meaningful order; (3) associate typewriter labels with the relevant graphical arrival/assembly and narrated fact, not arbitrary simultaneous reveals; (4) inspect actual intermediate decoded frames during path drawing, docking, label reveals and transitions for collisions, clipping, premature reveals and incorrect sequencing, then inspect settled states. Preserve contextual scene variety, rounded geometry, independent editable layers, subtle lighting, existing V6 branding and +64px caption/progress positions. Do not force timelines or T-shaped diagrams into unrelated stories; use story-appropriate geometry. Inter fallback and simplified dissolve are implementation limitations, not new brand rules.

**MANDATORY — typewriter and frame-level QA correction, 24 November 2026:** Every newly appearing editorial text element, including kicker, headline from its first character, graphic labels, callouts and chart annotations, must visibly type on (no cursor). Numeric counters retain their count animation. Fixed logo/topic pill and word-follow captions retain their existing approved behavior. Historical dissolve-delay rule superseded on 26 November: type visibly from scene start; never hide the first word in the transition. Directional movement accompanies typing. Growing chart fills must redraw rounded geometry at every intermediate width, not crop a completed rounded bar. Check overlap throughout movement, not only final stills; reroute paths and reposition labels away from artwork. No rectangular hero background panel, including full-width panels that visually enclose artwork; open-canvas artwork remains mandatory. Include provisional centered, single-line word-follow captions in music-only review cuts using the approved script and explicitly estimated timing; replace with real acoustic timing before final delivery. Maintain compact visible-phrase backing, 24px radius, no shadows or blur. Inspect actual decoded video frames during transition, intermediate bar growth, and settled state; image-generation contact sheets are not render verification.

**MANDATORY — independent graphic and text motion; owner correction 24 November 2026:** Never flatten the main artwork, labels and statistics into one animated rectangle or move a complete static hero as a substitute for motion design. Build meaningful separate layers: every story text element (headline, kicker, value, callout, label, supporting text) has its own eased entrance/reveal; fixed brand avatar/topic pill are intentional chrome exceptions. Chart bars/lines grow progressively, numeric quantities count toward verified end values, modules/tiles assemble or stagger in, paths draw/connect sequentially and nodes appear with appropriate timing. Continue subtle contextual motion with travelling light pulses, illuminated LEDs, circuit traces or other relevant effects after reveal; avoid flashing, unnecessary ornament or obscuring text. Keep distinct contextual backgrounds, 60fps motion, V6 captions and quiet transitions. Before owner review inspect native decoded frames during reveal, after reveal and near transitions for each scene, including full glyph bounds, artwork/text clipping, safe margins, actual within-hero movement (not merely background or progress bars) and settled end values. Fix clipping at source; do not mask over pixels or defer a known visible defect to the next review.

**MANDATORY — distinct scene backgrounds, owner correction 24 November 2026:** Each narrative scene must have a visibly different story-specific background composition and contextual motion, not a common decorative base with a changed color, gradient, glow or repeated curve. Theme colors may repeat; the artwork, geometry, depth and movement must change to support the content. A continued beat inside the same scene may retain its environment for continuity. V6 locks the branding/chrome (logo, topic pill, typography, captions and centered chapter progress), not background artwork. Before storyboard delivery, assign a unique scene-level background design ID and show actual native-resolution frames; audit the contact sheet for repeated geometry, not just palette, and verify distinct scene background layers in editable source. Before draft delivery, inspect representative rendered frames and within-scene background motion at native 60fps. If this gate fails, correct the source and regenerate before review. Do not reintroduce the prototype V6 radial glow + ellipse as a universal background. Do not include editorial reference labels.

**MANDATORY on-screen text rule — owner instruction, 24 November 2026:** Do not add source/reference/editorial meta labels in video frames, storyboards, title cards or hero graphics. Remove phrases such as “Illustrative server architecture,” “Government-reported snapshot,” “Mission-wide figures,” “Source: ...,” report citations, internal evidence labels and production notes. Keep references, provenance, explanatory context and asset-rights notes in the research record and YouTube description. Preserve material factual qualifiers directly in normal story content (e.g. date of measured data, ordered versus deployed, under manufacture, National Supercomputing Mission versus PARAM Rudra) whenever omitting them would mislead. Use clearly schematic artwork rather than simulated real facility footage; where an illustration or synthetic media disclosure is necessary to avoid misleading the audience, use appropriate disclosure, not a generic on-screen reference line. Apply this during storyboard generation and visual QA before rendering. No change to approved V6 chrome, captions or progress bars.


**MANDATORY — camera-space isolation, real geographic data and continuous layout QA (owner feedback BM-007, 28 November 2026):** Use purpose-driven camera movement on artwork when it aids understanding (e.g. zoom to the requested starting end, track along a growing measurement ruler in the owner-approved direction, return to full view; map overview, focus on first city, pan/focus second, return to overview; central subject stays continuous while camera pulls back to reveal a group). Do not transform fixed brand chrome or captions. Hide initial editorial heading and obsolete labels before expanded artwork enters their space; restore only after returning to safe bounds. Keep independent metric text in separate screen-space lanes outside the maximum transformed artwork extent, including rulers, connectors and animation offsets. Owner refinement BM-007 V4: for maps, city names must be in genuinely empty space (above the corresponding shipyard name), with a thin leader from the exact projected geographic pin when needed; do not put text on land geometry or coastlines. Test the entire reserved callout bounds including glyphs, descenders and icons against the actual projected map polygons and coastline stroke, not only screen bounds; route leaders to stop short of text, and verify both callouts and leaders against each other throughout the camera move. Fade before intersection.  keep city, yard, count and their associated graphics in the same moving geographic layer. Inverse-scale anchored typography as needed for readability; gate labels by transformed bounds and retain both locations in the final wide view. Avoid an inset rectangular clipping edge during geographic camera motion; use the full canvas and soft edge fades to protect fixed chrome and captions. Verify camera direction explicitly against owner instruction and growing ruler direction. No unexplained polygons or arrows. Use actual sourced geographic vectors projected consistently with verified city coordinates; never hand draw substitute national borders. For India prefer official Survey of India data; if inaccessible use a documented geographically sourced India-claimed-extent vector, disclose source, generalization, boundary perspective and rights limitations, and verify against official references before publication. Preserve a single subject through zoom rather than crossfade duplicate graphics. Gate surrounding objects on full transformed bounds, not merely their scheduled entrance; closing labels must remain below maximum fleet extent. Mandatory regression checks at every provisional frame time for camera clip/text lanes, glyph bounds, headline opacity, subject bounds and camera endpoints; also render and visually inspect decoded intermediate frames. XML validity, unique IDs, a contact sheet of settled frames or frame count alone do not establish absence of collisions. Fix every known collision before presenting a revision.
