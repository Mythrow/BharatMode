# ID-005 — GalaxEye orders Indian-built propulsion for 30 satellites from Bellatrix

**Stage:** In Progress (high priority); production code BM-004. **Event/source date:** 24 November 2026. **Registered:** 25 November 2026. Category: Space & Exploration.

## Verified news
At India International Space Conclave 2026, Bellatrix Aerospace announced a multi-year contract to supply ARKA Hall-effect electric propulsion systems for GalaxEye’s next 30 Drishti Gen-II OptoSAR satellites. Bellatrix says design, manufacture and testing will take place in Bengaluru, and the propulsion will move satellites to their intended imaging orbits after launch. Company describes the order as the largest by satellite count between two private Indian space companies since 2020 reforms; attribute that superlative to the company, not independent proof. ARKA was qualified in orbit on PSLV-C58 in 2024, according to Bellatrix. No contract price verified. Not 30 satellites launched or delivered; no confirmed delivery/launch dates. No claim that all components indigenous. Distinct from prior AI cluster, supercomputing, and medical stories.

Technology: Hall-effect thrusters ionize propellant gas and accelerate ions using electric fields; magnetic field influences electrons; low thrust over long periods and higher propellant efficiency versus typical chemical engines. Not propellant-free, not a launch rocket. Actual gas, model, thrust and power for this contract undisclosed. Bellatrix ARKA product page lists family (discharge power/thrust/specific impulse): 50 W/3 mN/860 s; 100 W/7 mN/1000 s; 200 W/13.2 mN/1300 s; 1.5 kW/86 mN/1600 s; 5 kW/260 mN/2000 s. These are manufacturer specs, NOT contract-specific. ISRO primary report 27 Jan 2024 confirms completion of ARKA200 xenon electric propulsion experiment and RUDRA green propulsion on PSLV-C58 POEM-3, launched Jan 1. Do not conflate RUDRA POEM-4 Jan 2025 firing with ARKA. Source https://www.isro.gov.in/ISRO_EN/POEM-3_Mission_achieves_Payload_objectives.html and https://bellatrix.aero/arka . NASA background https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/in-space_propulsion/ .

OptoSAR: optical multispectral cameras capture reflected sunlight and spectral/visual detail; active synthetic aperture radar transmits microwaves and measures echoes, can image at night and through most cloud cover. Combine complementary data on same platform, not optical camera seeing through cloud. Planned imaging applications agriculture, flood mapping, infrastructure and environmental monitoring; not demonstrated Gen-II performance. ARKA intended to move from rideshare injection to assigned imaging orbit, maintain spacing, enable collision avoidance and end-of-life maneuvers, subject to mission design and remaining propellant.

Important context: maiden Mission Drishti launched May 3 2026 on Falcon 9; GalaxEye July 7 statement reported loss of contact after anomaly following geomagnetic storm, recovery unlikely.
